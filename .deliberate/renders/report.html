Water-dynamics comparison: main · xpbd · twofield

Captured 2026-06-18 · WebGPU canvas via agent-browser · scenes approximately (not pixel-) matched across the two codebases.

All three water-look failures are one dissipation knob — how much a particle's velocity is pulled toward the local average (PIC / XSPH) vs kept as its own momentum (FLIP / ballistic). On a grid the grid velocity is the local average, so the PIC fraction and XSPH viscosity are the same knob. Each solver keys it differently:

main (old MPM) — low dissipation (ballistic preserve up to 0.95–0.98) keyed on support_ratio. Splashy, but slow sparse drips get near-max preserve in the cup → FLIP energy the projection amplifies → cup stir / blow-up.
xpbd (PBF) — tuned local dissipation (XSPH viscosity, lower at the surface) + position-based constraint (no projection feedback). Splashy and stable. The reference.
twofield (current) — max dissipation (G2P velocity is pure grid-gather; pic_blend only damps the affine C). Stable but slime / no splash.

⚠ Cross-codebase caveat. main is hardwired to the V60 cone/cup rig — every main frame shows that geometry, never a plain box. So on scenes 1 (box slosh) and 4 (sand wall) the main row is only a loose water-character reference (geometry differs: main's "slosh" is water in the V60 cup; its "sand wall" is water-over-bed in the cone, and effectively static). main's cup blow-up (which you observed live) did not reproduce in the benchmark center-pour here — water is held in the cone over the grounds rather than accumulating in the cup; capturing it needs a cup-prefill scene main doesn't ship. The fully apples-to-apples comparison is xpbd vs twofield (same harness, all four scenes). main's value here: its free stream + cup pool read as coherent/lively (the "good water" character) even on the rig.
