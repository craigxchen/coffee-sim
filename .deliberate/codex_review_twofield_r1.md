REVISE

1. **KTD-2 gates do not fully prove `A = D·G` in the operator actually used.** Adjointness of `D/G` and MMS on `D/G/L` are necessary, but not sufficient. The gate must explicitly test the assembled projection operator used in the solve: `A = -D M^{-1} G`, including mass/volume-fraction weighting, boundary masks, free surfaces, restriction/prolongation, and Neumann/Dirichlet rows. Otherwise a self-consistent `D/G` pair can pass while the coarse/fine seeded solve injects pressure with a different operator.

2. **The divergence-decay gate is gameable because it measures with the same divergence operator the projection uses.** That catches the old exact floor only if the implementation error is inside `D/G`; it does not catch a physically wrong projection that hides divergence in surface cells, cut cells, φ gradients, or boundary flux. Add independent volume-change gates: particle density/volume drift, tank water height, flux through closed walls, and free-surface fill-fraction conservation.

3. **The coarse seed can miss or corrupt low-frequency modes near boundaries.** The plan assumes the coarse solve kills low modes, but restriction/prolongation around solid cells, free surfaces, V60 geometry, and bubble constraints is exactly where multigrid-like schemes often fail. Add spectral/two-grid convergence tests on representative masked domains, not just boxes: closed tank, sloped wall, V60 cone, partially filled bed, and a cavity/bubble case.

4. **Fine local Jacobi after a coarse seed is not obviously enough for visual incompressibility.** Fixed Jacobi smoothing damps high-frequency residual slowly for variable-coefficient and free-surface Poisson problems. The plan needs pre-registered absolute tolerances tied to visible density/free-surface error, plus a two-grid convergence factor gate on known eigenmodes. “≥2× per budget doubling” is weak; a solver can satisfy that while still requiring too many sweeps for 33 ms.

5. **KTD-4 is directionally correct but underspecified for saturated porous media.** The continuity constraint should come from phase volume conservation: `∂φ_f/∂t + ∇·(φ_f v_f) = source`, `∂φ_s/∂t + ∇·(φ_s v_s) = source`, with their sum constrained only under the correct saturation/source assumptions. The plan states `∇·(φ_f v_f + φ_s v_s)=0`, but does not clearly handle evolving φ from compaction, swelling, absorption, and drainage as source terms in the projection.

6. **“Apply `-∇p` φ-weighted to both velocity fields” is too loose and can be wrong.** Pressure force is volume-fraction weighted in momentum, but velocity acceleration depends on phase mass: roughly `-(φ_k ∇p)/(φ_k ρ_k)` where φ does not simply multiply the velocity correction away. In the rigid-skeleton U6 case, the skeleton should not be projected into motion; pressure should become a reaction/effective-stress term. This needs equations, not prose.

7. **The KTD-4 discrimination gates are not sufficient to pin the formulation.** Submerged-block buoyancy can pass via an added buoyancy force. Undrained pore-pressure rise can pass with a tuned pressure source. Terzaghi consolidation is stronger, but it appears only in U7 after many dependencies. Add earlier U6/U7 gates for Skempton `B`, consolidation under multiple drainage lengths, load sharing between effective stress and pore pressure, and φ-change-driven pore flux.

8. **KTD-3’s exponential drag fold is only exact for frozen linear drag.** Gidaspow/Ergun/Wen-Yu β depends on φ, slip speed, Reynolds number, and possibly permeability evolving during the step. A per-node exponential update is still useful, but the plan overclaims unconditional stability. It needs gates for nonlinear drag under high slip, dry-to-wet transition, and φ near packing where β changes sharply.

9. **The drag/projection Lie split is a real instability path.** The plan names Strang/Aitken/virtual mass, but treats them as levers rather than gates. At density ratio 0.83 with stiff drag, pressure projection and drag should be tested as a coupled partitioned system: oscillation amplitude, energy growth, and phase-slip decay under timestep refinement. Otherwise “stable drag” can still produce unstable drag-pressure splitting.

10. **Solids pressure and pore pressure coupling can double-count resistance.** U5 has a grid-level over-packing solids-pressure term; U7 adds Bishop effective stress and pore pressure. The plan needs a precise stress decomposition so granular contact pressure, pore pressure, cap pressure, and projection pressure do not all resist the same volumetric mode.

11. **The bed model is plausible but the performance premise is under-defended.** One 3x3 SVD per solid particle per substep in WGSL may be fine at 12k solids, but L3 says 200k particles without clearly bounding solid count, substeps, or plasticity frequency. Repose ≥30° is plausible with Drucker-Prager, but only if boundary friction, grid resolution, φ cap, and damping do not dominate the heap metric.

12. **U6 parallel with U5 hides coupling risk.** Rigid-skeleton drag/projection can run before plasticity, but it will not expose failures caused by moving φ_s, compaction-driven pore pressure, effective stress feedback, or drag on a deforming skeleton. That is acceptable for early risk reduction, but the plan should not imply U6 validates the two-field formulation beyond the frozen-bed limit.

13. **Several gates still allow tuning to pass.** Cavity depth/persistence, pond depth/drain time, repose, and crater depth all depend on knobs that are not fully pre-registered. The knob-grid rule is good, but it must include pressure tolerance, coarse ratio, sweep count, timestep/substep cap, suction parameters, drag blend width, solids-pressure stiffness, plastic damping, and boundary/filter coefficients before implementation.

14. **The 33 ms @200k target is not obviously impossible, but the plan lacks a dispatch/substep budget.** Two P2G scatters with fixed-point atomics, two G2P gathers, pressure coarse/fine passes, bubble classification, drag, φ/K updates, suction/absorption, SVD plasticity, and possibly CFL substeps can easily exceed budget. Require a per-rung maximum dispatch count, memory bandwidth estimate, and substep ceiling before L1/L2 investment.

15. **The plan should add a hard conservation gate for pressure work.** Projection, drag, suction, absorption, swelling, and plasticity all exchange momentum/energy. Momentum conservation is mentioned, but pressure impulse transfer between phases and boundaries needs accounting: fluid, solid, absorbed mass, open drains, and wall/filter reaction forces should balance separately.
