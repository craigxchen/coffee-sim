# Re-architect the realism core to two-velocity-field PB-MPM (stiff grid, no Poisson) — requirements

## Summary

The coffee-pour water must **bounce/scatter on impact** and read as real water, while keeping the two assets the current two-field solver has — the **coffee/water coupling** and the **frame rate** (real-time, ~200k particles / ≤33 ms). A long patching campaign on the two-field grid solver (surface-weighted dissipation transfer, density-relief/density-target pressure, merge discriminator) did **not** deliver realistic water. The converged diagnosis: **a bounce is a stiff-incompressibility (pressure) response — the pool must spike pressure and shove the impacting water back** — and the two-field's **under-converged velocity-divergence (`∇·v`) Jacobi projection is fundamentally soft** (it's fast *because* it under-converges, which is the same reason it can't bounce). A fully-converged grid Poisson would bounce but is slow (`main`'s sparse-RBGS Poisson runs 40–100 red/black pressure pairs and its pressure-pair loop plausibly dominates the frame — exact share pending a checked-in profiler artifact, see Sources).

**Decision: re-architect the realism core to two-velocity-field PB-MPM** (Position-Based MPM — the SOTA fan-out's candidate **A**). PB-MPM keeps everything **on the grid** (the faster path measured here today: two-field runs 25 ms@207k on a full coupled scene vs the XPBD particle core's ~90 ms@200k *extrapolated water-only with optimization levers still open* — see R2/KD8 for why this proves "grid is faster today," not that particle XPBD is eliminated) and replaces the soft `∇·v` projection with a **compliant, position-based density constraint** (the XPBD/PBF mechanism). The bet is that this gives **stable local stiffness at a few iterations — enough for a visible bounce/push-back — without a full fine-grid Poisson**. It does **not** claim to deliver globally-converged stiff incompressibility for free: low-frequency density information still has to propagate across the domain, which is why KD2 carries an explicit **coarse-grid pressure pre-pass** (an approximate global low-frequency solve — cheaper than a full Poisson, but global pressure work nonetheless, to be measured not assumed). XPBD is the in-repo existence proof that few-iteration compliant position constraints bounce in real time. The coffee/water **coupling is rebuilt** on this substrate: two co-located momentum fields, a pore-packing density target, Laibe-Price drag, XPBI bed plasticity, and conserved absorption — patterns proven in pieces across XPBD and the two-field. Whether the constraint multiplier `λ` can be converted into a usable pore-pressure field for buoyancy/Terzaghi is a **hypothesis to validate (KD5)**, not a settled mechanism. The assembled two-velocity *porous* PB-MPM has no shipped reference (EA SEED's PB-MPM is single-phase water); that novelty is a **risk to retire by building**, separate from the validated repo physics it ports.

This supersedes the two-field-patching path; the two-field, `main` MPM, and XPBD become reference/referee solvers (and donors of portable pieces + the validation harness).

## Problem Frame

- **Bounce = stiff incompressibility.** Water bounces/scatters off the cup/pool only if the fluid is stiff enough that an impact spikes pressure and reflects the incoming momentum. XPBD and `main` MPM bounce; two-field does not — it "just pools where it is" (visual oracle). Empirically, every two-field water failure (mushy/no-bounce, over-pack, over-buoyant coffee, settled stirring) traces to its `∇·v` projection: it's under-converged (residual compressibility), it doesn't even constrain density, and the projection dissipates the impact momentum that should bounce.
- **The perf↔stiffness trap on the classical grid.** Converge the Poisson → stiff but slow; under-converge → fast but soft. Two-field chose fast/soft. `main` chose a converged-ish RBGS Poisson (40–100 red/black pressure pairs; the pressure-pair loop plausibly dominates its frame — soften pending a checked-in profiler artifact, see Sources) with an operator-inconsistency divergence floor. Neither gives bounce **and** speed.
- **PB-MPM relaxes (not escapes) the trap.** It replaces the soft `∇·v` projection with a **compliant position-based density constraint** (XPBD-style): the compliance gives a *stable, stiff restoring force* on density error at a few iterations (→ local push-back → visible bounce) without converging a fine-grid solve. This buys **stable local stiffness**, not globally-converged incompressibility — the EA PB-MPM work itself notes Jacobi-style parallel constraint solving converges slowly / stays artificially soft, and presents *stability at any stiffness*, not exact stiffness, as the win. Globally-stiff 3D incompressibility still needs low-frequency information to cross the domain; KD2's coarse-grid pre-pass supplies that as **approximate global low-frequency pressure work** (cheaper than a full fine Poisson, but not free, and to be measured). The net perf claim is that this is cheaper than `main`'s fine RBGS Poisson, *not* that global pressure work disappears. EA SEED ships the single-phase core real-time in WebGPU.
- **The grid is the perf path here.** Measured in-repo: two-field grid = 25.4 ms@207k; XPBD particle core = ~90 ms@200k (linear after the reorder, levers unpulled). Staying on the grid (PB-MPM) keeps the frame-rate asset; a particle core would have to claw back ~3.6×.
- **The coupling must survive the move.** Coffee/water coupling (infiltration/saturation, buoyancy, crater, Terzaghi, conserved absorption) is an asset and is conservation-critical. It is the part PB-MPM must **rebuild** (the largest scope + novelty risk), because PB-MPM's incompressibility is position-based (it emits position/displacement corrections), not the cell-centered `∇p`/`G·p` the two-field coupling consumes.

## Key Decisions

- **KD1 — Position-based compliant density constraint replaces the `∇·v` projection.** It targets **density** (J / volume) directly, so it cannot suffer the two-field's density-blind over-pack, and the compliance gives a *stable* stiff restoring force at a few P2G→solve→G2P iterations. Expected outcome: stable local stiffness sufficient for a visible bounce/push-back — **not** a guarantee of globally-converged stiff incompressibility at that iteration budget (a parallel Jacobi-style constraint solve converges slowly across the domain; bounce is a local response, global incompressibility is not). Globally-stiff behavior is reconciled with speed by KD2's coarse pre-pass, not by the constraint alone. (Existence proof for the *local* claim: XPBD/PBF bounces real-time today with this mechanism.)
- **KD2 — Stay on the grid; port EA SEED's WebGPU PB-MPM as the single-phase water reference, plus a coarse-grid pressure pre-pass.** Honors the frame-rate asset and gives a concrete reference for the water core. The pre-pass is an **approximate global low-frequency pressure solve** (a small multigrid-ish / coarse-level helper) — it is global pressure work, just on a coarse level, so the perf claim is "cheaper than a full fine-grid Poisson," not "no global solve." Its structure, level count, iteration count, and measured cost are an open planning question (see Outstanding Questions). This is the one substantively "unbuilt piece."
- **KD3 — Two co-located momentum fields (water + solid) for percolation.** Water and grains move at different velocities in the same cell. Laibe-Price analytic exponential drag (exact at any stiffness, O(1)/pair) folds into the constraint iteration.
- **KD4 — Mixture incompressibility: water density target `ρ₀·(1−φ_s)` (pore-packing).** Per-cell volume fractions `φ_s` (solid) and pore fraction `n = 1−φ_s`; saturation `s = φ_f/n`. Water packs into the pore space rather than expelling grains. (Proven in XPBD's coupling — `targets ρ₀·(1−α_s)`.)
- **KD5 (HYPOTHESIS, not assertion) — `λ` may be convertible into a pore-pressure field.** *Correction:* XPBD does **not** take `∇λ` of a smooth grid field. In `src/solvers/xpbd/coupling.wgsl`, it forms a per-particle scalar `pressure_for_water(i) = max(-lambda[i], 0)` (coupling.wgsl:228-230) and then accumulates **pairwise SPH-gradient impulses** over neighbor water/grain pairs (`spiky_grad`, coupling.wgsl:257-258, :274+) — particle-side, compression-negative, not a cell-centered pressure gradient. So the original "exactly as XPBD derives buoyancy from `∇λ`" was wrong. The real decision: a PB-MPM per-cell multiplier *may* be usable as an algorithmic pore-pressure for buoyancy/Terzaghi **only after** a sign / units / smoothness / iteration-dependence / stress-partition audit (the 2026-06-17 doc already warned PBF `λ` is particle-side and compression-negative, not directly portable to the grid pressure contract). If the audit fails, buoyancy falls back to an XPBD-style pairwise impulse from `max(-λ,0)` or an explicit hydrostatic term. The over-buoyant-coffee issue is *expected* to resolve here as physical buoyancy, but that is the thing to prove, not assume.
- **KD6 — Conserved absorption/wetting, ported.** Water↔grain transfer with `water_lost == grain_gained` exactly + effective mass for momentum (swelling), from XPBD `wetting.wgsl` / two-field U9. Conservation is gate-enforced (volume + total-water + the long-run saturated-tail regime).
- **KD7 — XPBI for the bed plasticity/crater.** Klar et al. Drucker-Prager return map run inside the position-based loop (XPBI, arXiv 2405.11694) — pressure-dependent yield, tamping memory, the crater that holds. Plus a small APIC affine momentum on the pour/free-water to sustain the cavity (the one thing pure position-projection dissipates).
- **KD8 — Perf is proven FIRST, and the prototype must bound the *assembled* system, not just single-phase water.** The first build is a minimal EA SEED single-phase water PB-MPM that measures **both the bounce and the frame rate at ~200k** before coupling. But a single-phase ≤33 ms result does **not** bound the full solver, which adds a second velocity field, drag, the coarse pre-pass, bed plasticity, wetting, extra storage buffers, and extra dispatches. So KD8 also requires a **two-velocity cost projection** alongside the prototype: either a stubbed second field + drag + λ ping-pong + bed/wetting buffer budget measured for per-pass cost, or a conservative additive estimate of each added pass's cost against the headroom. The go/no-go bar is the *projected assembled* frame time clearing ≤33 ms@200k, not the single-phase number alone.
- **KD10 — Storage-buffer and fixed-point budgets are decided up front, not discovered.** The device requests **exactly 9** storage buffers per stage (`src/utils/gpu.rs:114`, `NEEDED_STORAGE_BUFFERS=9`), and gpu.rs explicitly warns that requesting more / deriving from `adapter.limits()` makes `request_device` **fail in the browser** — so anything above 9 needs a deliberate `GpuContext` limit-change *and* live browser verification (the 16 figure in `AGENTS.md` is the hardware cap, not what's currently granted; two-field's widest entry point binds 7, `common.wgsl:9`). The plan must carry a **buffer/pass ledger** for two momentum fields + `λ` (ping-pong) + bed state against both the current 9 and the sanctioned 16, splitting passes if needed. Likewise **fixed-point headroom**: the existing FP_SCALE/overflow math (`common.wgsl:124`) is for mass/momentum accumulation lanes, *not* accumulated position corrections / PB-MPM displacement state — a new overflow surface that needs its own headroom gate.
- **KD9 — Build along the validation ladder.** Isolate each interaction, then pairwise, then all three (per the SOTA program): water-only (bounce + perf) → + bed plasticity (crater/repose) → + two-field percolation/drag → + saturation/absorption → full coupled pour-over. Each rung gets its own gates; the existing render harness + Debug Scenes catalog + the twofield/xpbd test families are the validation substrate.

## Requirements / Success Criteria

- **R1 — Water bounces/scatters on impact** (crown/rebound off the cup floor, walls, and standing pool), lively free surface, not "pools where it is." Primary oracle: the live webapp on the comparison scenes. Quantitative gate (non-gameable): a **localized, mass-weighted post-impact rebound/spread** metric (upward+outward free-surface momentum or rebound height in the impact neighborhood) that materially exceeds the two-field baseline, **guarded by** a low cap-hit rate (the rebound must not be a clamp artifact), a no-popcorn check (no single-particle ejecta spikes), and conservation (no mass/energy injected). Noisy global upward motion must not satisfy R1.
- **R2 — Real-time:** ≤ 33 ms @ ~200k on the fixed-point i32 grid (no float atomics), under Tint uniformity, within the granted storage-buffer budget (see KD10). The perf premise is "grid is the faster substrate *measured today*" — two-field's 25 ms is a full coupled saturated-pour scene (`docs/PERF_NOTES.md`, `tests/twofield_perf.rs`), whereas XPBD's ~90 ms is extrapolated water-only with levers still open; this justifies *starting* from the grid, not a final elimination of the particle architecture. **Verified by the KD8 prototype + two-velocity cost projection before the full build.**
- **R3 — Coffee/water coupling at two-field fidelity, conserved** — each sub-claim its own gate, no blanket "fidelity": (a) infiltration/drawdown rate within a plausible band vs the two-field/XPBD reference; (b) saturation field tracks `s = φ_f/n`; (c) **grains seat under a standing water column** (a calibrated no-over-buoyancy gate — the current failure mode, must be measured not assumed resolved); (d) a crater that **holds** under wet-sand plasticity (absolute pit depth persists, per the existing crater-persistence gate); (e) Terzaghi/Skempton effective-stress partition (drained weaker than flooded contrast probe); (f) mass/volume conserved (free + absorbed water, `water_lost==grain_gained`), gate-enforced incl. the long-run saturated tail.
- **R4 — Incompressibility is stiff and density-targeting:** density histogram bounded (ρ/ρ_rest, no over-pack/dense-ring), volume conserved, settled-pool tail KE bounded (no spontaneous stirring), and the pool reflects impacts (the R1 rebound gate). "Reads incompressible" alone is not the gate — the density/volume/tail-KE/rebound quartet is.
- **R5 — The architecture is a coherent whole** (one solver, the three interactions coupled), not a patchwork; the two-field/main/xpbd remain as reference/referee solvers and as the source of portable pieces (XPBI, conserved wetting, Laibe-Price drag, the render harness).

## Candidate Approaches

- **A — Two-velocity PB-MPM (chosen).** Grid, position-based compliant density constraint (stable stiff push-back at few iterations + a coarse low-frequency pre-pass instead of a full fine Poisson), two momentum fields + `λ`-as-candidate-pore-pressure + Laibe-Price drag + XPBI bed + conserved wetting. Pros: best reconciliation of bounce + measured-today frame-rate + grid-coupling; EA SEED single-phase water reference to anchor the core. Cons/risks: the two-velocity *porous* mixture has no shipped reference (rebuild + research risk); the coarse-grid pre-pass is unbuilt; `λ`-as-pore-pressure is unproven (KD5); perf of the *assembled* system is unproven (KD8 + the two-velocity cost projection retire it); it must beat candidate D on the merits.
- **B — Particle (XPBD) core + grid-assisted coupling (rejected, but not decisively eliminated).** Proven bounce + the coupling is more preserved (XPBD already has pairwise λ-impulse coupling), but XPBD is ~90 ms@200k today and a particle↔grid handoff adds cost/complexity. *Caveat:* the 90 ms is extrapolated water-only with optimization levers explicitly unpulled (`docs/PERF_NOTES.md`, `[[project_perf_target]]`), so it is **not a fair floor** — B loses on *measured-today* perf and on rebuild scope, not on a proven impossibility. Retained as the primary fallback if KD8 fails.
- **C — Keep patching the two-field `∇·v` projection (rejected).** Soft by construction (the bounce↔convergence trap); the patching campaign empirically failed.
- **D — Compliant predicted-density-error projection on the *existing* two-field operator (rejected, but the closest rival).** The 2026-06-17 brainstorm (`docs/brainstorms/2026-06-17-twofield-water-compliant-density-projection-requirements.md`) proposed a compliant density-error target on the current cell-centered operator — distinct from C (it *does* target density, not `∇·v`) and lower-risk (it preserves the `G·p` cell-centered pressure contract the bed coupling already consumes, and reuses the whole two-field substrate + gates). The reason to prefer full PB-MPM despite D: that doc's compliance-diagonal variant was reviewed as harmful/uncapped-relief-prone, and a projection-on-the-old-operator still inherits the under-converged-Jacobi softness D's own gates struggled with (the DensU thread has ~unresolved gates today). **But this is the candidate the new doc must beat on the merits** — full PB-MPM is justified only if the position-based constraint genuinely bounces where D's projection stays soft, *and* the coupling-rebuild cost (PB-MPM emits position/displacement corrections, while the bed coupling consumes `G·p`) is worth paying. If the KD8 prototype shows PB-MPM doesn't out-bounce a compliant projection, D (cheaper, no coupling rebuild) wins.

## Scope Boundaries

**In scope:** a re-architected two-velocity PB-MPM realism core — the position-based density constraint (water incompressibility/bounce), the coarse-grid convergence pre-pass, two momentum fields + Laibe-Price drag, the pore-packing mixture (`ρ₀·(1−φ_s)`, saturation), `λ`-based buoyancy/Terzaghi, conserved wetting/absorption, XPBI bed plasticity/crater, APIC-for-cavity; the KD8 perf prototype; the validation ladder gates.

### Deferred to Follow-Up / Build-Order
- Full coupled pour-over fidelity is the *last* rung; early rungs isolate water-only, then bed, then percolation, then absorption.
- Surface tension / sub-grid droplets (not required for bounce/liveliness).

**Outside this effort's identity:** the two-field / `main` / XPBD solvers are **not** modified to become the primary — they stay as reference/referee + donors of portable pieces; the just-built transfer work (U1–U3, uncommitted on `feat/twofield-surface-weighted-dissipation`) is superseded (kept as learnings). No **full fine-grid** Poisson is introduced (the point is to avoid the fine RBGS cost); the KD2 coarse pre-pass is global low-frequency pressure work on a coarse level, which is in scope.

## Outstanding Questions (for planning)

- **The KD8 perf prototype is the gating first step** — exact scene/particle count, the bounce metric, the pass bar (≤33 ms@200k), AND the two-velocity cost projection method (stub vs additive estimate). If the projected assembled cost fails, the architecture is reconsidered (fallback: candidate D — compliant projection on the two-field operator, no coupling rebuild; then candidate B; then accept two-field for coupling-only).
- **Does PB-MPM out-bounce candidate D?** The KD8 prototype should include (or be quickly comparable against) a compliant-density-projection variant on the existing two-field operator, so the choice of full PB-MPM over D rests on a measured bounce difference, not assumption.
- **The coarse-grid pressure pre-pass** for PB-MPM 3D convergence: structure, level count, iteration count, and **measured cost** (it is global low-frequency work — its cost must be in the KD8 budget, not hand-waved as "small").
- **The conservation handshake** in the two-velocity mixture (P2G/G2P adjointness, volume/momentum conservation through the constraint loop + the wetting transfer) — port the two-field/XPBD conservation gates; watch the reorder stale-phase artifact.
- **Fixed-point i32 mapping** of the position-based constraint (`λ`, position deltas) on the no-float-atomics grid; FP overflow headroom for *position/displacement* lanes (a new surface — the current `common.wgsl:124` math covers mass/momentum only); storage-buffer ledger (current granted **9**, sanctioned **16**) for two momentum fields + `λ` (ping-pong) + bed state, with a `GpuContext` limit-change gate + live browser verification if >9 (per KD10).
- **Migration shape:** a new solver in `src/solvers/` registered alongside the existing three (the registry pattern), reusing the scene/render/test harness.
- **What ports vs rebuilds:** XPBI (Klar return map), conserved wetting, Laibe-Price drag, the Debug Scenes + render harness are portable; the incompressibility core + two-velocity mixture + `λ`-coupling are new.
